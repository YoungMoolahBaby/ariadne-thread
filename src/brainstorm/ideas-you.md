# Anchored in you

Ideas whose trust comes from your own habits, memories and checks. They cost nothing to build and work everywhere. Their weakness is the [fifth threat](../problem/threat-models.md#5-your-own-mind): they depend on the very mind the simulation affects. So they are best as layers on top of an outside anchor, not as the only thread.

## Y1. The totem

*Job: verify · Restores: layer · Today: yes*

A small object, or a check, with one property only you know: a coin with an unusual weight, a ring with a scratch in a particular place, a phrase only you and one other person share. In the real world it behaves the way you know it does; a world that does not know the property gets it wrong.

- **Why:** straight from *Inception*. It works because the answer lives in your memory and the object, not in the world's model.
- **Protects against:** drift, careless worlds, hostile content that does not know your secret.
- **Weak spot:** physical totems only work where you can feel real objects. A system that reads expectations, or a future one that reaches memory, could defeat it. Telling anyone, or using it in a recorded space, spends it.
- **Build it:** today. It is a habit plus an object.

## Y2. Reality-check habits

*Job: verify · Restores: layer · Today: yes*

Lucid dreamers train themselves to check whether they are dreaming several times a day: read a line of text, look away, read it again; check a clock twice; look closely at their hands (see [dreams](../research/dreams.md)). The simulation version would use the checks simulations still get wrong: fine detail at very close range, the weight and texture of a familiar real object, your own hands up close, sound in a corner of the real room.

- **Why:** a habit runs without a decision. If it is practised often enough, it happens even when you are absorbed. VR users already do a folk version: some touch objects after a session "to reaffirm they are indeed real" (Porter & Robb 2022).
- **Protects against:** drift, layer confusion.
- **Weak spot:** in dream research, reality testing *alone* is the weakest induction technique (Aspy et al. 2017), so treat it as a supplement. Each check also expires as simulations improve: people currently tell real from virtual memories partly by rendering flaws (Bonnail et al. 2024). The list needs revising, and the core principle is to check things *the world has no reason to have modelled*.
- **Build it:** today. The research question is which checks still work, on which hardware, and for how long.

## Y3. A letter from before

*Job: guide, verify · Restores: self, time, state · Today: yes*

Before going in, record a short message to yourself: where your body is, what time it is, what you are doing next, how you feel, what matters to you today. On the way out, the [airlock](ideas-platform.md#p6-the-airlock) plays it back.

- **Why:** it re-anchors self, time and plans in one step, in your own voice. It is also continuity: the person who went in speaking to the person who came out.
- **Protects against:** drift in self and time; it softens aftereffects.
- **Weak spot:** a hostile system could replay or fake it. Its value is re-orientation, not security.
- **Build it:** a voice memo app. The interesting version is automatic: the platform asks for it at entry and plays it at exit.

## Y4. Re-entry checklist

*Job: guide · Restores: place, time, self, state · Today: yes*

A short checklist after every session, done the same way every time, like a pilot's after-landing checks:

1. **Where am I?** Look around the real room. Find the door.
2. **When is it?** Real clock. How long was I in?
3. **How is my body?** Thirsty, hungry, stiff, needing the toilet, steady on my feet?
4. **Am I me?** Name, plan for the rest of the day, mood.
5. **What happened outside?** Messages, people, anything left running.
6. **Am I fit to drive, cook, climb stairs?** If unsteady, wait.

Ending with a grounding routine borrowed from anxiety therapy, such as naming five things you can see, four you can touch, three you can hear.

- **Why:** checklists work because they do not rely on judgement in the moment. Military simulator guidance has long told pilots to wait before flying or driving after a session.
- **Protects against:** drift and aftereffects.
- **Weak spot:** people skip checklists when they feel fine, which is exactly when drift goes unnoticed.
- **Build it:** today, on a card by the headset, or built into the airlock.

## Y5. Before and after

*Job: verify · Restores: self · Today: yes*

A thirty-second self-check before going in and after coming out: a few questions about mood, how real things feel, how connected you feel to your body. Over weeks, the record shows whether you are coming back as the person who went in.

- **Why:** drift in the self is slow and invisible from inside. A record catches what memory smooths over. Research on dissociation after VR already uses short questionnaires of this kind (see [aftereffects](../research/aftereffects.md)).
- **Protects against:** slow drift in self, the threat no device addresses.
- **Weak spot:** self-report is noisy; people will skip it.
- **Build it:** a two-minute form at session start and end.
