# What a good thread must do

This chapter turns the problem, the research and the lessons from elsewhere into a checklist. Each idea in the [catalogue](ideas.md) is judged against it.

## Ten requirements

Most of these come straight from Theseus's thread (see the [introduction](../introduction.md)).

| # | Requirement | From | Means |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Anchored outside** | The thread tied at the door; *pebbles, not bread* | The thread's record and its exit do not live in the content it leads you out of |
| R2 | **Continuous** | No gaps in the thread | Every transition (into an app, a world, a nested layer) is captured; there is no moment where the trail stops |
| R3 | **Tamper-proof, or at least tamper-evident** | The labyrinth could not change the thread | The content cannot edit, move or remove it, or if it can, you can tell |
| R4 | **Usable when impaired** | Followed by touch in the dark | It works without reasoning, reading or remembering much: physical, rehearsed, one action |
| R5 | **Shows depth** | The length of thread unwound | You can tell how far, how many layers and how long |
| R6 | **Two-way** | Someone waiting at the other end | You can signal out, and the outside can signal or pull you in |
| R7 | **Invisible until needed** | (The seatbelt) | It costs little presence while you are not using it |
| R8 | **Always reachable, never punished** | The fire exit | Leaving is never blocked, delayed or penalised by the content |
| R9 | **Works across every layer and maker** | (Nesting) | One thread through all nested layers, whoever built them |
| R10 | **Brings back the self, not only the body** | (The five starting points) | It restores place, layer, self, time and state, not only position |

## Three jobs

A thread can do three different things. The difference turns out to matter more than any other.

- **Guide**: help you find the way back. A path, a direction, a sequence of steps. (The thread itself.)
- **Verify**: help you know you are back, and which layer you are in. (The totem.)
- **Extract**: bring you out whether or not you find the way or notice you need to. (The kick; someone pulling the rope.)

Guiding and verifying both work by *informing* you. Extracting works by *acting on* you. From the [philosophy chapter](../elsewhere/philosophy.md): against a simulation that can show you anything, information can be faked and action cannot. And against your own confused mind (threat 5), information may not be used at all. So:

> **A complete thread needs at least one extractor anchored outside the content.** Guides and verifiers make the way back pleasant and confident. The extractor makes it certain.

## Five anchors

Where the thread is tied determines what it can survive.

| Anchor | Example | Trust depends on |
|---|---|---|
| **In the content** | A glowing trail drawn by the game | The content being honest and working |
| **In the platform** | The headset's system menu, which apps cannot block | The operating system being honest |
| **In separate hardware** | A watch tapping your wrist; a cable; a physical switch | That device, which the content does not control |
| **In other people** | A friend in the room who can call you or lift the headset | That person being present, attentive and trusted |
| **In you** | A habit, a check, a rehearsed routine, a memory | Your mind working normally, the thing most at risk in threat 5 |

Anchors in the content are convenient and fine for drift; they are *bread*. Every other anchor is some kind of *pebble*. The [comparison](comparison.md) shows the pattern: the worse the threat, the further outside the content the anchor must be.

## A shape for the whole thing

Pulling it together, a full thread is probably not one device but a layered system, the way building safety is exits plus alarms plus drills plus fire wardens:

1. **A trusted exit** the content cannot block (platform plus hardware): the extractor.
2. **A record of the way in** kept outside the content: the guide and the depth gauge.
3. **A transition back out** designed to restore place, layer, self and time: the airlock.
4. **Checks and routines** you practise until they are automatic: the verifier in you.
5. **Someone at the other end** when the stakes are high: the extractor of last resort.
