# Open questions

Questions this notebook cannot answer yet. Each could become a search, a prototype or a study.

## About people

1. **How often do people actually get lost today, and in which of the five ways?** There is research on exits and aftereffects, but very little data on everyday sessions at home.
2. **Who holds the other end when you are alone?** The anchor person is the strongest thread and the least available. What is the best approximation without a person?
3. **Can a thread stay invisible without being forgotten?** If it never intrudes, will people remember to use it when they need it?
4. **Does rehearsing the exit lower presence?** Pilots train emergency procedures without enjoying flying less. Is the same true here?
5. **How do you notice drift in yourself?** The self is the least served starting point. What would a good measure of "came back as myself" look like?
6. **What about people who do not want to come back?** A thread serves the person's choice. How should it behave when someone, in their usual frame of mind, chooses to live mostly in a simulation?

## About systems

7. **Who keeps the stack when layers come from different makers?** A world inside a world inside a platform: which one is responsible for the return stack? Is there a standard to propose, for example as an OpenXR extension?
8. **What is the VR version of the line of death?** Browsers keep a strip of screen that web pages cannot draw on. Headsets have no such strip: the content owns every pixel, and the immersive web "has no concept of a browser chrome" (Hosfelt et al. 2020). Is there an equivalent area, sense, device or moment in time that only the platform may use?
9. **In a direct neural interface, is there any channel the simulation does not own?** Perhaps the body's internal senses, perhaps a separate physical interlock. Or perhaps none, leaving only people and hardware outside.
10. **Can mixed reality show which things are real?** As passthrough and rendering converge, should rendered objects be marked, the way synthetic images are starting to be watermarked?

## About rules

11. **Should there be a right to exit?** Buildings must have fire exits, and the exits must not be locked. Should simulations be required to honour an exit within a fixed time, and never penalise leaving?
12. **Who may pull you out?** If a sitter, a buddy, a parent or a platform can extract you, how is that power limited? An extractor is also a way to interrupt someone against their will.
13. **What should a simulation be allowed to do to your sense of place?** Redirected walking is harmless in a game. The same technique could be used to lose someone on purpose.

## About the research itself

14. **What has already been tried in high-stakes simulation?** Flight simulators, military training, and medical VR may have practical exit procedures that never reached academic VR research.
15. **What do people with long VR habits already do?** Communities of very heavy users may have invented their own threads. That is folk knowledge worth collecting.
