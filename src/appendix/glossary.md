# Glossary

**Aftereffects.** Effects that persist after leaving a simulation: unsteadiness, eye strain, altered perception, a sense of unreality.

**Airlock.** In this book, a standard gradual transition between a simulation and the real room (idea [P6](../brainstorm/ideas-platform.md#p6-the-airlock)).

**API layer (OpenXR).** A library that sits between OpenXR apps and the runtime and can observe or modify their calls. A natural place for a thread that works across apps on PC.

**Break in presence.** A moment when someone who felt present in a virtual place suddenly becomes aware of the real one.

**Clew.** A ball of thread; the origin of the word *clue*, via the story of Theseus.

**Depersonalisation / derealisation.** Feeling detached from oneself (depersonalisation) or feeling that one's surroundings are unreal (derealisation). Common briefly in many people; persistent forms are a clinical condition.

**Extract, guide, verify.** The three jobs a thread can do: bring you out, help you find the way, help you know you are back. See [requirements](../brainstorm/requirements.md#three-jobs).

**False awakening.** Dreaming that one has woken up. The dream version of the fake exit.

**Full dive.** A hypothetical interface that feeds the senses directly through the nervous system, replacing them completely. Does not exist.

**Game transfer phenomena.** Experiences after gaming in which elements of the game carry over into everyday perception, thoughts or actions, such as seeing game images or reaching for game controls.

**Guardian / boundary / chaperone.** The headset feature that shows the edges of a safe play area, and usually the real room, when you get close to them.

**Home vector.** In animal navigation, the internally computed direction and distance back to the nest. In this book, the platform's knowledge of the real way back to the start spot (idea [P8](../brainstorm/ideas-platform.md#p8-real-home-vector)).

**Layer.** One level of simulation. A game inside a social world inside a headset's home environment is three layers deep.

**Line of death.** In browser security, the boundary between the browser's own trusted interface and the web content area, which pages cannot draw above.

**Line arrow, cookie, REM.** Cave-diving markers on the guideline. Arrows point toward the exit and can be read by touch; cookies are non-directional personal markers; REMs (referencing exit markers) are directional only to the diver who placed them.

**Lucid dream.** A dream in which the dreamer knows they are dreaming. Reality testing is a common training technique for inducing one.

**Measured boot.** A computer security technique in which each piece of software loaded at start-up is hashed into a register that can only be extended, never rewritten, so the history can be checked later.

**Mixed reality (MR).** Displays that combine the real environment with rendered content, usually through camera passthrough on headsets.

**Out-of-band.** Using a separate channel from the one being checked, such as a code sent to your phone to confirm a login on your computer.

**Passthrough.** Showing the real environment inside a headset via its cameras.

**Path integration.** Keeping track of position by adding up one's own movements, without landmarks.

**Pebbles, not bread.** This book's main rule: a trail made of the world's own material can be eaten by the world.

**Place illusion and plausibility illusion.** Slater's two components of presence: the feeling of being in the place shown, and the feeling that what is happening there is really happening.

**Presence.** The feeling of being in the virtual place rather than in the real room.

**Reality monitoring.** The mental process of telling whether a memory came from something perceived or something imagined.

**Reality anchor.** A cue from the real world kept visible inside VR (term from Bajorunaite et al. 2023). A thread is a reality anchor that is always available, cannot be removed by the content, and leads somewhere.

**Redirected walking.** A technique that makes people walk a different real path from the one they see, usually by rotating or scaling the view by amounts too small to notice.

**Rule of thirds.** Cave-diving gas planning: a third of the gas going in, a third coming out, a third kept for emergencies. Turn around when the first third is used.

**Secure attention sequence (SAS).** A key combination the operating system reserves so that programs cannot intercept it, guaranteeing that the user is talking to the real system. On Windows, Ctrl+Alt+Del.

**Shadow stack.** A protected second copy of a program's return addresses, kept by the processor so that an attack cannot redirect where a function returns to.

**Substitutional reality.** A virtual environment built so that each real object in the room has a virtual counterpart in the same place.

**Tell.** In this book, a sign of the medium (weight, lag, edge of view, the cable) that reminds you which side of the boundary you are on. See [why better simulations make it harder](../problem/why-harder.md).

**Transitional interface.** An interface that moves the user between points on the real-to-virtual spectrum, rather than switching abruptly.

**Trusted path.** A channel between the user and the system that untrusted software cannot imitate or intercept.
