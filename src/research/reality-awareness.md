# Keeping the real world in view

A large body of work asks how to let some of the real world into VR while you are still inside it. A thread is, in a sense, the minimum amount of reality that must always be available.

## Letting reality in

- **McGill et al. (2015), "A Dose of Reality"**, showed real objects (keyboard, drinks, people) inside VR. Showing reality *only when the user engaged with a real object* was best for both performance and presence.
- **Hartmann et al. (2019), "RealityCheck"**, composited a live 3D reconstruction of the real room into existing games without changing them, tested with seven titles and frequent VR users. It shows reality can be blended in at the system level, outside the app.
- **Simeone et al. (2015), "Substitutional Reality"**, gave every physical object a virtual stand-in in the same place, and studied how much mismatch people tolerate. The real room as a constant, disguised anchor.

## Reality anchors

**Bajorunaite et al. (2023)** coined the term **reality anchors**: cues from the real world kept visible inside VR, studied for headset use on public transport. Other people and one's own belongings were the most useful anchors, and the need for anchors grew when users had to manage their own journey.

The term fits this book closely. A thread is a reality anchor with three extra properties: it is always available, it cannot be removed by the content, and it leads somewhere.

## Bystanders and interruptions

- **O'Hagan et al. (2023)** found VR users choose how to be made aware of bystanders based on social context and how much immersion they want to keep, more than on usability.
- **Gottsacker et al. (2021)** found that showing a real person who interrupts as part of the virtual scene (diegetically) gave the best interaction and the least disruption. They introduce a questionnaire for cross-reality interruptions (the CRIQ).
- **Ghosh et al. (2018), "NotifiVR"**, surveyed 61 VR users and built a framework for auditory, visual and haptic notifications in VR.

## What this means for the thread

1. **Reality on demand beats reality all the time.** McGill's result suggests a thread should stay quiet until engaged, which matches the "invisible until needed" requirement.
2. **System-level blending works.** RealityCheck did it without app cooperation; so can a thread (see the [thread layer prototype](../brainstorm/prototypes.md#1-the-thread-layer-pc-vr)).
3. **Personal belongings are good anchors.** Bajorunaite's finding supports the [totem](../brainstorm/ideas-you.md#y1-the-totem) and [home base](../brainstorm/ideas-platform.md#p7-home-base).
4. **Diegetic is comfortable but untrustworthy.** Drawing reality *as part of the scene* is least disruptive, which makes it right for gentle reminders. But anything drawn as part of the scene is drawn by the scene, and can be faked by it. The trusted exit signal must be the opposite: clearly *not* part of the world. Gentle reminders can be bread; the exit itself must be a pebble.
