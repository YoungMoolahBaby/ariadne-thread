# Dreams: reality testing and false awakenings

Every night, people enter a fully convincing simulation, generated by their own brains, and leave it again. Dream research is the longest-running study of the layer problem.

## Reality testing

Lucid dreamers practise **reality checks** while awake (reading text twice, checking a clock, looking at their hands) so the habit carries into dreams, where the check fails and reveals the dream.

The evidence is weaker than its popularity suggests:

- **Stumbrys et al. (2012)**, a systematic review of 35 studies, found the methods generally of low quality, and concluded that **none of the induction techniques were verified to induce lucid dreams reliably and consistently**.
- **Aspy et al. (2017)** (N=169) found **reality testing alone was the weakest technique**. Combining it with waking briefly in the night and a memory-based technique (MILD) gave the clearest increase.

## False awakenings

A **false awakening** is dreaming that you have woken up: the dream version of the fake exit.

- **Buzzi (2011)**: among 90 lucid dreamers, 41% had false awakenings at least monthly, and 46% had had one in the past week.
- **Buzzi (2019)**: false awakenings often occur together with lucid dreams, and a reality check inside a false awakening can lead to lucidity.

## Signalling out of a dream

- **LaBerge et al. (1981)** had lucid dreamers send agreed eye-movement signals from inside verified REM sleep. It was the first proof that someone inside a fully convincing inner world can send a message to the outside.
- **Konkoly et al. (2021)** went further: real-time two-way dialogue between experimenters and people dreaming. Experimenters asked questions (for example, simple sums) and dreamers answered with eye movements. Correct answers came from a minority of attempts, but the channel worked.

## What this means for the thread

1. **Fake exits are natural, not only an attack.** False awakenings are common in people who dream lucidly. Nested realities produce fake exits by themselves, with no adversary needed. A thread must handle them as a normal case.
2. **Reality checks alone are weak.** The habit that lucid dreamers rely on is the least effective technique on its own. [Y2](../brainstorm/ideas-you.md#y2-reality-check-habits) should be a supplement, not a foundation.
3. **A thread from inside to outside is possible even in the deepest layer.** LaBerge and Konkoly show a two-way channel between a person in a self-generated world and someone outside, using a signal (eye movements) the dream does not override. This is the strongest precedent for the [anchor person](../brainstorm/ideas-people.md#o1-the-anchor-person) and the [call-back signal](../brainstorm/ideas-people.md#o2-the-call-back-signal), and for choosing a channel the inner world does not control.
