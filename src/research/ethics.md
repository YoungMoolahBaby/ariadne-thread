# Ethics: the right to leave

## What has been written

- **Madary & Metzinger (2016)**, "Real Virtuality: A Code of Ethical Conduct", is the main reference. It flags the risks of long-term immersion and of behaviour changes that persist after leaving a virtual environment, and recommends informing users and funding further research.
- **Slater et al. (2020)**, "The Ethics of Realism in Virtual and Augmented Reality", warns that virtual experiences may become indistinguishable from real ones, and works through worst-case scenarios.
- **Tseng et al. (2022)** and **Bonnail et al. (2023)** show concrete ways perception and memory could be manipulated in extended reality (see [wayfinding](wayfinding.md) and [presence, time and memory](presence-time-memory.md)).

## What has not been written (as far as this pass found)

This first research pass found no standard, regulation or industry guideline that **guarantees an exit**: that requires a simulation to let you leave within a fixed time, forbids penalising you for leaving, or requires the platform to keep a way back that content cannot block. Platforms do this in practice to varying degrees (see [what headsets ship today](products.md)), but as product decisions, not obligations.

*To do:* a dedicated search of standards bodies (IEEE, ISO, the OpenXR working group), consumer-protection rules on dark patterns, and platform store policies.

## The building-code analogy

Buildings are required to have exits. The exits must be marked, lit, unlocked from inside, and never blocked, and the rules exist because people in a fire do not think clearly. Nobody considers fire exits an insult to the building.

A "right to exit" for simulations could follow the same pattern:

1. **Always available**: a way out that no content can block or hide ([H1](../brainstorm/ideas-hardware.md#h1-the-arch), [P1](../brainstorm/ideas-platform.md#p1-the-exit-contract)).
2. **Clearly marked**: you can always find it, even confused.
3. **Never locked from inside**: no penalty for using it ([P10](../brainstorm/ideas-platform.md#p10-leaving-is-free)).
4. **Drilled**: practised until automatic.

## The other side: who may pull you out

An extractor is also an interruption. If a sitter, a buddy, a parent, an employer or a platform can pull you out, that power can be misused: to cut someone off, to monitor them, to control what they experience. And if [virtual realism](../elsewhere/philosophy.md) is right, life inside a simulation can be a real life that someone has chosen.

A fair principle: **the person sets the terms of their own thread, beforehand, in their usual frame of mind**. Who may pull, under what conditions, with what limits. The thread serves that choice; it is not a tool for others to override it, except in narrow emergencies agreed in advance.
